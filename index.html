Motivation

Why I do this.

I have had two obsessions my whole life: gaming and psychology. This is what happens when you combine them.

  • I study the gap between what people say and what they do.Most human behaviour runs on autopilot. My interest is in making the invisible visible: finding the patterns in what people do and giving teams something they can actually act on.
  • The counterintuitive questions are the interesting ones.Why does removing a feature players hated make them angrier? Why does anticipating a reward feel different from receiving it, even when the brain responds the same way?
  • Games are a unique research environment.High-stakes emotional behaviour (frustration, trust, flow, social dynamics) in a controlled setting. The stakes feel low to the participant. The insight is anything but.
  • I've experienced gaming communities at scale.Attending major esports events shapes how I think about player psychology, not just as data points, but as something felt firsthand.

VR Cognitive Impairment Simulation

UE5Custom VR build
1 of 4Cognitive domains prototyped
ProposalProof of concept

Designed and built a proof-of-concept VR environment in Unreal Engine simulating alcohol-like visual impairment, proposed as part of a larger study into how impairment affects cognitive performance. The prototype demonstrated one of four planned cognitive tests, introducing measurable perceptual distortions during an in-world task with a single demonstration participant to show how the full study would run.

Scoped as a four-domain study examining distinct cognitive capacities and how they interact under impairment. Delivered as an academic presentation covering design rationale, methodology, and implications for accessibility and safety simulation.

Motor Learning in DCD: Gamified Research with Children

First Class Honours
Design challengeDesigning for a 9 to 11 year old participant group meant the research instrument had to work as a game first and a study second, an alien-feeding task standing in for a visuomotor rotation test, with findings analysed in Jamovi and written up with implications for inclusive, child-appropriate UX methodology.
6Pilot studies
100Trials per participant
9-11 yrsParticipant age

Investigated motor learning differences in children with Developmental Coordination Disorder using a gamified visuomotor rotation task, presented as an alien-feeding game for participants aged 9 to 11. Piloted across 6 runs of two 50-trial stages each, the study compared implicit and explicit learning strategies across DCD and typically-developing groups.

Designing research instruments for a child population required careful attention to ethics, age-appropriate language, and engagement, skills directly transferable to game UX research with diverse player populations.

UX Analysis

Game design critiques.

Independent UX and player psychology analysis of AAA titles, applying behavioural frameworks to real game design decisions. These sit alongside the case studies as evidence of how I think, not just what I have done.

Counter-Strike 2

A Tale of Two Economies

A psychological critique separating CS2's two design systems: a skill-based in-game economy built on prospect theory and cognitive load, and a monetisation economy built on variable reinforcement, the near-miss effect, and classical conditioning. Examines the ethical implications of gambling mechanics in a free-to-access platform that peaks at 1.8 million concurrent players, including the CSGOLotto scandal and the gap between regulatory response and psychological evidence.

"The skin economy isn't cosmetic. It's a second game built on variable reinforcement, running parallel to the first."

League of Legends

Mastery, Monetisation, and Manufactured Toxicity

A three-part critique examining how League of Legends sustains 180 million players through emergent complexity and staged mastery; how the ranked system satisfies self-determination theory needs while weaponising loss aversion and the goal gradient effect; and how Hextech monetisation deploys variable ratio reinforcement and engineered FOMO against a predominantly adolescent player base. Argues that toxicity is a structural output of anonymous high-stakes design, not a player personality problem, and that Riot built the conditions for it.

"Toxicity isn't a player personality problem. Riot built the conditions for it."
